How to find your wage determination

The wage determination sets the minimum base rate and fringe for every trade on a Davis-Bacon project. Here's how to find yours on SAM.gov, make sure it's the right version, and use it to check your certified payroll.

Check your payroll against it

Paste the rates into the free WH‑347 form and every worker is checked as you type.

Start with your contract

The wage determination that applies is the one included in your contract or subcontract, usually in the specifications or an attachment. Its number looks like MN20260001 (state, year, sequence), followed by a modification number such as Mod 3. If your contract lists one, use that exact number and modification. You don't get to pick a newer or older one.

Finding it on SAM.gov

  1. Go to SAM.gov and open Wage Determinations.
  2. Choose Davis-Bacon Act (construction), not the Service Contract Act.
  3. Search for the number from your contract. Without a number, filter by state, then the county where the work is, then the construction type: Building, Heavy, Highway or Residential.
  4. Open the determination and download it. Archived versions are under its history if your contract names an older modification.

Which version applies

DOL publishes changes to wage determinations weekly, usually on Fridays, and new editions each year. Under 29 CFR 1.6(c), a revision issued before the contract is awarded generally applies. For sealed bids, a revision issued at least 10 days before bid opening applies; one issued in the last 10 days still applies unless the agency records that there wasn't time to tell bidders. A revision issued after award generally doesn't apply, except when the contract is changed to add substantial work, extended, or an option is exercised. When in doubt, ask the contracting agency or your prime contractor, who has to give subcontractors the same determination.

Reading the rates

Classification
The trade, sometimes split into groups (for example, LABORER, GROUP 2). Each worker must be paid for the work they actually did.
Rates
The minimum hourly base rate.
Fringes
The minimum hourly fringe benefit. You can meet it with contributions to a bona fide plan (column 6B on the WH‑347), in cash (6C), or a mix.
Union or survey
Codes at the start of a line show where the rate came from: a union agreement or a DOL wage survey. It doesn't change what you owe.

If a classification your crew needs isn't on the determination, the contracting agency has to request an added classification (a conformance) from DOL. Don't pick the closest-sounding trade on your own.

Check every worker against it

On EasyWH347, open "Check pay against the wage determination" and paste the rate lines from the determination. Each worker's base rate and fringe are compared with their classification before you download, so an underpayment shows up on your screen instead of in the agency's review. It's free and nothing is uploaded.
